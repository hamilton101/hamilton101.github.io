Holographic and light-field displays can present three-dimensional scenes with
natural motion parallax, but the large number of views and pixels required by
these displays creates major challenges for video capture, representation,
storage, transmission, and real-time rendering.

This project develops representations and processing systems that make
high-quality holographic video practical. Current research directions include:

- light-field video representation and compression;
- real-time rendering and selective decoding;
- frame translation and view synthesis;
- capture, display simulation, and quality evaluation; and
- immersive viewing across holographic, virtual-reality, and conventional displays.

The work builds on the lab's research in cinematic light-field video,
multi-gigapixel rendering, holographic display simulation, and viewer-centred
quality assessment.
